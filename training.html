How-to · livestock guardian dogs

You do not teach a Pyr to guard. You raise the instinct so it can go to work.

Training means bonding, boundaries, and calm corrections — then waiting for adulthood.

Disclaimer. Field guide drawn from parent-club, university, and conservation-district teaching. Not veterinary advice. If a dog is injuring stock or people, get help the same day.

1. Get the right animal first

  1. True LGD breeding. Great Pyrenees or another recognized guardian breed.
  2. Working parents if the job is real.
  3. Age window. 7–10 weeks. Bonding is much harder after about 16 weeks.
  4. One or two dogs, not a crowd. Adjust for terrain and predator load.
  5. Either sex can work. Buy the temperament in front of you.

2. Bonding, not obedience school

Food, calm praise, and a scratch should happen with the animals, not on the porch as a prize for leaving them.

  1. Small bonding pen with four to six quiet, dog-wise sheep or goats.
  2. A safe nook and feeder the stock cannot wreck.
  3. See, smell, hear, and sleep beside that species every day.
  4. Brief house time for handling is fine. Living on the sofa is how you get a kitchen alarm.
  5. An older opposite-sex worker is the best teacher.

3. Age ladder

Stage one

8–16 weeks

Small pen. Correct chase and mouth instantly. Reward lying down with stock.

Stage two

4–6 months

Bigger paddock. Feeding station stays in the livestock area.

Stage three

6–18 months

Many Pyrs are not reliable until about 18 months. Do not leave a teenager alone with fragile newborns.

4. Commands and night work

  1. No once, then redirect. Come at feeding. Stay when you leave — reward the pup that remains.
  2. Night work is disruption, posture, then chase. Do not raise a silent guardian.
  3. Do not house-raise and dump at six months. Do not reward leaving stock. Fence the territory.
